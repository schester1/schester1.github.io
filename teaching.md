<h3>Juneau Icefield Research Program (JIRP)</h3>
<p>
<a href="https://www.juneauicefield.org/">JIRP</a> is an undergraduate and graduate science immersion program that focuses on alpine glaciology and cryosphere science. I am a member of the Academic Council which designs and implements the eight-week undergraduate course curriculum. I was teaching faculty on ice in '25 and '26 where I led workshops, hands-on activities, and lectures on a range of topics including glacial geomorphology, sea level change, glacial isostatic adjustment, and GPS surveying. My primary goal at JIRP is to engage students in the scientific process, encourage them to ask questions and think critically, and inspire them to pursue their own interests. 

Every year, JIRPers present their work at AGU. Stop by some of their <a href="https://www.juneauicefield.org/agu">presentations/posters</a> to see the amazing work being done on the icefield.
</p>

<h3 style="margin-top: 2em;">Columbia University</h3>

<p>
I was a teaching assistant for three courses in the Department of Earth and Environmental Sciences: Solid Earth Systems, How to Build a Habitable Planet, and Sea Level Change. In addition to grading, I led lab activities and discussion groups on a range of topics. For me, being a TA is largely about office hours and one-on-one support. I enjoy helping students engage with the material and work through homework/lab problems. 
</p>
